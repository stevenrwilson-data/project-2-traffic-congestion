        F1         1                         SUPPORTED                  headline                           Sustained congestion and abrupt breakdown are empirically different traffic phenomena on this corridor.     The sustained-state rule produced 1457 events, the LA abrupt-collapse rule 10, and the Caltrans spatial rule 7, with extremely low pairwise onset overlap.                The definitions answer different operational questions rather than competing to identify one universal event set.
        F2         2                         SUPPORTED                  headline                                   Corridor-level abrupt-breakdown episode construction is robust to the temporal clustering rule.                                                                                   15-, 30-, and 45-minute clustering windows all produced 6 corridor episodes.      Only six corridor episodes were identified, so the result is strong for this sample but not a universal traffic-flow claim.
        F3         3                         SUPPORTED                  headline                                            Midday sustained-congestion episodes last substantially longer than AM or PM episodes. Median durations were 50 minutes AM, 170 midday, and 45 PM. The midday advantage remained positive after every leave-one-station and leave-one-date exclusion.      Time-period comparisons are observational and may reflect demand, geometry, incidents, or recurring operational conditions.
        F4         4                        SUGGESTIVE       headline/supporting                                                            Sustained congestion is often persistent and has a long duration tail.                                                                                        Median duration was 60 minutes and the 90th percentile was 355 minutes.              The sustained-state rule is an operational definition, and some episodes are right-censored by the analysis window.
        F5         5             SUPPORTED_WITH_CAVEAT                supporting Most corridor propagation classifications are stable, but spatial extent is not completely insensitive to the allowed lag window.                                                                4/6 episode classifications were unchanged across 30-, 60-, and 120-minute propagation windows. Episodes 3 and 5 changed classification; the 60-minute window is retained because it rejects clearly delayed 110-minute matches.
        F6         6                  SUPPORTED_METHOD methodological/supporting                                              Using 100% observed coverage instead of 80% costs no selected-corridor observations.                                                                           80% and 100% thresholds retained 164657 rows and 2540.1 simultaneous corridor hours.                                                              This equivalence is specific to the selected five-station corridor.
        F7         7                        SUGGESTIVE                supporting    Five minutes before abrupt collapse, matched-control differences are modest rather than a simple dramatic precursor signature.                                                                Event/control medians were 58.10/61.95 mph, 419.0/443.5 veh/5 min, and 0.1087/0.1150 occupancy.               The abrupt-onset sample contains only 10 events and 40.0% occur on the single most concentrated date (2026-03-02).
        F8         8             SUPPORTED_WITH_CAVEAT                supporting                             Recovery does not follow one universal station-order direction across the observed corridor episodes.                                                                    Episode-level travel-order recovery correlations include both positive and negative values.                                       Only propagated episodes under the primary 60-minute rule are included in this comparison.
        F9         9 UNSUPPORTED_FOR_SUBSTANTIVE_CLAIM                audit-only                                The current product-limit probability curve should not be used as a substantive portfolio finding.                                                The maximum estimated probability is only 0.0188% and 4 of the 10 pre-breakdown flows are below 600 veh/h/lane.
